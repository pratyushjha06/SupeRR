# PRESSURE-X Phase 4.6 — Combined IPL/T20I Candidate Evidence Review

## Purpose and decision
Phase 4.6 examines whether the pooled Top-20 candidate pools have meaningful pressure exposure in both IPL and T20I, and makes the format split visible for each candidate. It is a diagnostic review, not a new scoring model and not a final Top 10 selection.

**Decision:** retain the existing pooled balanced rating as the primary ranking evidence for now. Add format exposure and format-specific performance deltas as mandatory candidate-review evidence. Do not average IPL and T20I ratings: each format rating uses its own robust z-score distribution and is therefore not on a shared scale.

## Data scope
- IPL seasons identified by competition names matching `IPL YYYY`, plus matches marked `T20I`.
- Matches in scope: 853
- Deliveries in scope: 194,014
- Deliveries excluded as non-IPL/non-T20I: 6,985
- Candidate pool: pooled balanced Top 20 batters and Top 20 bowlers from the preceding validated analysis.

## Format coverage diagnostic
A candidate is labelled `both_formats_30plus` if they have at least 30 pressure balls (batters) or legal pressure balls (bowlers) in each format. This is a transparent exposure marker, not a replacement for the project's overall eligibility criteria. `single_format_30plus` means one format has at least 30 pressure balls while the other has less than 30. The threshold is intentionally descriptive and does not silently change the Phase 4 eligibility rule.

- Batters: 6 of 20 have 30+ pressure balls in both formats; 14 have 30+ in one format.
- Bowlers: 5 of 20 have 30+ pressure legal balls in both formats; 15 have 30+ in one format.

## What this means
1. The pooled candidate pool is not automatically format-balanced. Most candidates meet the 30-ball descriptive exposure marker in only one format.
2. A pooled rating is still defensible as a combined-evidence ranking if the report explicitly states that exposure is driven by the available competition data and does not claim equal IPL/T20I representation.
3. Format-specific metric deltas in the companion CSVs/workbook should be used to inspect whether the player's pressure-vs-normal pattern is directionally consistent. Do not compare the numerical `format_rating` values across formats.
4. A candidate with limited exposure in one format should be described as having insufficient evidence there, not as having failed in that format.
5. Do not force a 50:50 format weighting. That would change the target estimand and could amplify small or sparse format samples. Any such model would need an explicit rationale and sensitivity analysis first.

## Recommended candidate-review protocol
- Keep pooled balanced rating as the provisional ordering.
- For each candidate, report pressure and normal exposure separately for IPL and T20I, along with format-specific pressure-vs-normal component deltas.
- Flag format concentration when one format contributes more than 80% of pressure exposure; this is a descriptive warning, not an automatic exclusion.
- Retain existing pressure/normal sample-size and reliability rules.
- Use year-level recurrence from Phase 4.5 as supporting evidence, not as a direct rating bonus.
- Only select final Top 10s after manual review of all candidate evidence and a documented rationale for every inclusion/exclusion.

## Limitations
- The available data pack defines the scope and coverage; this analysis does not independently verify that every real-world IPL/T20I match in the date range is present.
- The 30-ball format marker is descriptive and is not a statistical power guarantee.
- Format-specific robust ratings are calculated within each format and are not comparable as raw magnitudes.
- Match context, role, batting position, bowling phase, opposition strength, venue and season conditions may still affect observed metrics.
- No official numeric evaluation rubric was supplied; no invented score or weighting has been applied.
